# Brief: Kopi tubruk

## The idea in one sentence

The bean is the constant from cherry to cup. Tubruk skips the filter, so the whole ground bean goes into the glass with the water, and the wait for the grounds to sink comes right before the sip. A pair of hands runs through every step.

## Format and spec

- Format: F2 morph chain, because the plot engine is a chronology (a real process in its real order). Each world closes to one shape that becomes the next world. F2 is proven in this skill (the 60s cut-paper history in `examples/history-of-ai`).
- Size: 1080x1920 (9:16, main) and 1080x1080 (1:1), 24fps, 120 BPM, 20s = 480 frames. Every cut and morph starts and ends on the 8th grid (0.25s = 6 frames).
- Style: `cut-paper`. Hero: none separate. The coffee bean (an oval with an S-crease) is the constant. Colour rule: the bean and the cherry are the only red-brown; the sun is yellow; no other colour is spent on the bean.
- Checklist deviation: cut-paper's checklist asks for a character with a face. The bean gets no face (per brief: no separate character). The recurring human thread is a pair of cut-paper hands, shown in shots 1 to 5 and 7. Recorded in LOG.md.

## Claims and sources (web-checked)

- Kopi tubruk is an unfiltered Indonesian coffee. Ground coffee goes into a glass, boiling water is poured over it, and the grounds are left to sink before drinking. Wikipedia, "Kopi tubruk" (https://Www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kopi_tubruk); Nescafe Indonesia guide (https://nescafe.com/id/en-id/coffee-culture/coffee-lifestyle/craving-kopi-tubruk).
- Grind size is disputed across sources (fine or coarse). The piece says "Pounded whole" and makes no grind-size claim. Jakarta Post, 2008 (https://www.thejakartapost.com/news/2008/05/10/enjoying-local-039mud-coffee039).
- Pounding in a mortar (lumpang and alu) is still done in some regions, and the grind is kept coarse for kopi tubruk. Regional article, Tulungagung "kopi kayu" (https://jatimtimes.com/baca/218473/20200712/153800/sensasi-kopi-kayu-tulungagung-rasa-nyampleng-harga-merakyat). Single source; the piece shows a mortar as a regional practice, not the only one.
- Traditional sangrai is done in a clay wok over a wood fire, stirred by hand, and it is slow and uneven. Indonesian academic papers and the same Tulungagung article (Bahasa Indonesia; the search returned abstracts and intros, not full methods). The piece shows a clay wok, wood fire and stirring, with no roast time or temperature.
- Coffee cherries turn red as they ripen, with variety exceptions (yellow or orange). Coffee Review (https://www.coffeereview.com/picking-ripe-not-as-easy-as-it-sounds/); PMC11243843 for the five colour stages (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11243843/).
- Sun drying is shown as a generic picture. No specific claim is made about it.
- Not claimed: "first", "oldest", "best", or any brand. Water temperature is not stated on screen (sources disagree).
